An empty folder is now a site that renders, bundles its own stylesheet, and keeps a sitemap you never wrote. That was the whole promise six pages ago, and it holds.

What you have is deliberately thin. Every part of it has a guide that goes further, and this page is the map to them.

---

## Next steps

**Another language.** If your site needs one, that is the [[multilingual-baseline-site | next page in this chapter]]. It picks up on the site you just built rather than starting over.

**The assets pipeline.** You wired a stylesheet in about five lines. The [[assets-pipeline | assets pipeline guide]] covers what happens either side of that: PostCSS on the way in, esbuild for scripts, and the inline filters for critical-path CSS.

**The head.** You dropped `<baseline-head>` into a layout and got a full `<head>` back. The [[head-and-noindex | head and noindex guide]] covers what lands in it, and how to keep a page out of search results when you want to.

**The sitemap.** It appeared without being asked for, which is the point. [[sitemaps-and-drafts | Sitemaps and drafts]] covers keeping pages out of it, and writing drafts that stay in dev.

**Deploying it.** The build you ran claimed an origin you passed on the command line. Making that true in production is [[deployment-url-checks | deployment URL checks]], and if the site lives under a subpath rather than at the root, [[deploy-under-a-subpath | deploying under a subpath]] is the one to read first.

---

## One thing worth knowing

The rules this tutorial set up live in a directory data file, not in each page's front matter. Adding a page means writing a `title`, a `description` and a `slug`, and letting the folder work out the rest. If you find yourself repeating `permalink` and `layout` on every page, the rule is not doing its job, and [[content-organisation | content organisation]] is where to look.

That is the shape of the whole thing, really. Decide once, in one place, then write pages.
